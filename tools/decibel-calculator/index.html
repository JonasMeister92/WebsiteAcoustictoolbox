Why decibels cannot be added arithmetically

A decibel is a logarithmic representation of a power ratio. Adding 70 dB and 70 dB as ordinary numbers would imply 140 dB, which is physically incorrect. The levels must first be converted to linear energy ratios, summed, and converted back to decibels.

Worked example

For two independent sources at 70 dB:

10 log10(1070/10 + 1070/10) = 73.01 dB

The combined level is therefore 3.01 dB higher than either source alone.

Background noise correction

A measurement often includes both the source and ambient background noise. Energetic subtraction estimates the source-only level. It is valid only when the measured total is greater than the background. When their difference is small, uncertainty becomes large.

Useful rules of thumb

Two equal levels
Combined level increases by approximately 3 dB.
10 dB difference
The stronger source dominates; the total rises by only about 0.4 dB.
Very small difference
Background correction is sensitive to measurement uncertainty.
