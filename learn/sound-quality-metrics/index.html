Understanding Sound Quality Metrics

Hear how roughness, sharpness and tonality change a sound, using controlled comparisons at similar listening levels.

01

Roughness

Fast amplitude fluctuations merge into a coarse or rattling sensation. Perception is strongest around modulation rates near 70 Hz.

Held constant
RMS level, duration and 70 Hz modulation rate
Changed
Modulation depth; the matched example also exchanges the 1 kHz carrier for broadband noise

02

Sharpness

Sounds dominated by high-frequency energy are perceived as sharper. The metric weights specific loudness toward high Bark bands.

Held constant
RMS level, duration, noise character
Changed
Spectral centre of gravity

03

Tonality

Tonality describes how strongly discrete pitches stand out from surrounding broadband sound.

Held constant
RMS level, spectrum range, duration
Changed
1 kHz tone-to-noise ratio

Reference values were calculated with MoSQITo from level-matched 70 dB SPL, 48 kHz excerpts. Playback level depends on your device, so the values describe the digital reference signals rather than the sound pressure at your ears. Roughness uses Daniel–Weber, sharpness DIN 45692 and tonality the ECMA-418-1 prominence ratio. Perfect isolation is not physically possible because psychoacoustic metrics interact.
